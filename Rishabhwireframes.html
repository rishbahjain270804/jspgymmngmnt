Who the product is for

Five roles, almost no overlap in what they need. The product is one backend behind five thin, role-specific frontends — which is also how it stays small. Nobody ever sees a feature that isn't theirs.

The rule that shapes every screen

A branch manager doesn't get a weaker permission than an Admin — they get the same permission at a narrower scope. So each screen is built once, and scope decides where you enter it: Admin lands on the all-branch roll-up, everyone else lands directly inside their own branch.

Check-in flow

The highest-frequency interaction in the product, and the one with the tightest budget: under two seconds, twice a day, at a counter with forty people waiting.

Why amber is not just "nearly red"

Three different situations produce amber — expiring soon, dues pending, wrong branch — and staff must be able to tell them apart at a glance. Each carries its own one-line reason and its own next action. An amber that just says "warning" makes the person at the counter guess, and they will guess wrong at 6:30 AM.

06

Wireframes

Deliberately greyscale and monospaced — these fix layout, hierarchy and content, not visual design. Colour appears only where it carries meaning: the green / amber / red status system.

Login

DemoAll roles

Phone plus OTP — never email. Many members and some staff have no email address at all. The role and branch come from the staff record, never from anything the client sends.

Check-in — the hero screen

DemoFront DeskFD-01 · FD-02 · FD-06

One screen, one job. The verdict block is enormous on purpose — it has to be readable across a counter, upside down, by someone who isn't looking directly at it. Desktop runs as a permanently-open kiosk; mobile uses the camera, which the web build can't match.

Dashboard — Admin, all branches

DemoAdminAD-01 · AD-02 · AD-04

Six numbers above the fold, then the branch comparison. The branch switcher and the cash/accrual toggle sit in the header and apply to everything below — they're not per-card filters.

Members — Level 1, the roll-up

DemoAdmin only

Total outside, branch-wise inside. This is the pattern every entity follows — Members, Staff, Equipment. A branch manager never sees this screen; they land directly on Level 2 for their own branch, because the aggregate is a permission rather than a page.

Members — Level 2, one branch

DemoFront Desk entry pointFD-03 · FD-07

Search is always phone-number-first — it's how staff and members both think about identity. Status colour is carried on every row so the expiring list needs no separate screen.

Member detail

DemoAll staff, scoped

Seven tabs, but front desk only sees four — Profile, Membership, Payments, Attendance. Progress, Workout and Diet require health access, which counter staff don't get. Medical conditions and body-fat numbers are not counter-desk data.

Collect payment

DemoFront DeskFD-04 · FD-05

Part payment is first-class, not an edge case — "₹3,000 now, ₹2,000 next week" is normal here and foreign software handles it badly. The GST split is computed and shown, never typed. Discount above the role's cap becomes an approval request rather than a silent giveaway.

Accounts — branch P&L

DemoAdminManager (own branch)AD-03 · BM-01

The most owner-impressive screen in the product, and the one no Indian competitor offers. Front desk can never reach it. Every figure drills to the source document — Tally's habit, worth copying exactly.

Equipment

DemoAdmin · ManagerAD-08 · FD-08

The clearest demonstration of the roll-up: total units outside, branch-wise inside, no explanation needed. Out-of-order count is really a churn indicator — broken machines are one of the top reasons members quit.

Progress & printable report

DemoCoach · ManagerCO-03 · CO-04 · ME-03

OAN already promises body-composition tracking on their website — this is that copy, implemented. The report is the commercial piece: a member shown measured proof of six months' progress renews far more readily than one asked cold.

Coach — today's clients & session log

NextCoachCO-01 · CO-02

Mobile-first by nature — a coach is on the floor with a phone in their pocket, not at a desk. Last week's loads are prefilled. That single decision is the whole design: a coach who must retype last week's numbers logs nothing, and an empty log makes every progress metric worthless.

The test to apply to every new screen

If a screen has more than seven things on it, it's the wrong screen. Advanced fields live behind "More", never on the default form. And the count that matters is per role — an Admin seeing six modules is fine as long as front desk still sees three.

Scope shown: the demo slice — Members, Plans, Check-in, Payments, Equipment, Accounts, Progress — across web, desktop .exe and mobile, with two branches and three role logins. Wireframes are layout and content only; visual design comes after these are agreed.
